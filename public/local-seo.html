Local search starts with the actual job.

A homeowner searching for hardwood installation needs to see the service, the area you cover, examples of your work and a clear way to request an estimate. A general page about your company rarely answers all four questions.

Burnworth Co. provides local SEO for owner-operated contractors and service businesses, with website implementation handled directly by Brandon Burnworth. The work connects what customers search for with the services you actually sell.

For a flooring business, refinishing an existing floor and installing a new one are different buying decisions. We plan the pages around those decisions, then use real projects to show where and how the business works.

What the work includes.

  • Search and competitor review: compare your priority services and actual service areas with the businesses customers encounter.
  • Service pages: explain suitability, process, material choices, project constraints and the next step.
  • Business information: reconcile your trading name, website, phone number and eligible business profiles.
  • Project evidence: use approved photographs and first-hand job details to substantiate your experience.
  • Technical implementation: fix crawl errors, page titles, internal links, canonical URLs and mobile usability issues.
  • Authority review: identify relevant trade directories, supplier listings and genuine editorial opportunities; assess them for audience and relevance.

Website changes and profile work are scoped together. Profile edits require access to the account that owns the listing.

Service areas need evidence.

A service-area page should help someone decide whether to hire you in that location. Useful details include actual projects, travel or scheduling constraints and the services available there. Changing a city name in otherwise identical text adds little.

For example, Top Tier Custom Floors has a Valley Center base and works in markets including Rancho Santa Fe, Carmel Valley, Carlsbad and Oceanside. The distinction between the business location and its service area matters. Neither a service-area page nor a schema field creates a physical office.

Google describes local rankings in terms of relevance, distance and prominence. Website work can improve relevance and support reputation, but it cannot change how far a business is from a searcher. Read Google’s local ranking guidance.

Measure the leads that matter.

We separate branded searches from people discovering the company for the first time. Reporting should connect service-page visits with genuine inquiries, the requested job and whether it fits the business.

A call-button click is an expression of interest, not proof of a completed call. A submitted form is not automatically a qualified lead. We agree on those distinctions before reporting growth.

Useful measures include non-branded search clicks, priority-page impressions, eligible profile interactions and qualified inquiries by service. Where local position tests are used, reports record the location and date.

Before we start.

Can you improve my existing website?

Yes. We first assess what can be retained. A redesign is only useful when the current site prevents needed improvements or no longer serves the business.

Do you guarantee first place in Maps?

No. The scope covers research, implementation and measurement. Search positions depend on competitors, proximity and search systems outside any agency’s control.

What access is useful?

The website, Search Console, analytics and an eligible Business Profile help establish a baseline. If tracking is missing, that becomes part of the initial scope.
