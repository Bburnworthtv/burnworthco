The story

Before search systems,
I built broadcast systems.

My career started in live television, where the problems are public, the deadlines do not move, and the system has to hold while millions of people are watching.

Since 2014 I have worked in broadcast engineering, robotic camera systems, IP video and remote production across some of the largest live shows on television — the Super Bowl Halftime Show, the Academy Awards, the Emmys, the Grammys, Dancing with the Stars, American Idol, America's Got Talent, the BET Awards, The Masked Singer and Love Island.

None of that work is visible to the audience, and that is the point. A signal path, a camera control system and a show file either hold up live or they do not. You learn to read the whole system before you touch any part of it, and to verify a fix instead of assuming it worked.

Burnworth Co. applies the same method to a different technical system: the one connecting a business to its customers through its website, search, and now the AI assistants people ask before they ever open a search results page.

What carries over

Different industry.
Same operating system.

Understand the system. Diagnose the failure. Implement the right change. Verify the result. It worked on a show floor and it works on a website.

01

Diagnose before changing

I do not open with a redesign or a keyword list. I find where the system is actually losing visibility, speed, trust or conversions first — that is what a visibility audit is for.

02

Build for real conditions

A site has to work on a phone on a slow connection, load quickly, say something clear, and be readable by machines as well as people. Not just look right in a mockup.

03

Measure the output

Rankings and AI citations are instruments, not the goal. I connect the technical work to calls, form fills and branded search, then check whether the change did what I said it would.
